NEW WORDS

take up phrase to fill up
The clothes take up room in the drawer.

matter n. solids, liquids, and gases
Rock is made of hard matter.

state n. a condition of a thing
Ice is the solid state of water.

solid n. a thing that has 3D qualities
This solid is a rock.

mass n. an amount or volume of matter
The mass of snow is very heavy.

liquid n. a thing that is able to move, such as water, milk, etc. Juice is a liquid.

container n. a thing that holds, such as a jar, glass, bowl, etc. Put the extra food in a container.

spread v. to move out or over a place
“Spread out so you have room, children,” she said.

Three Types of Matter

All things are made of matter. Matter is anything that takes up space. Look around at the things in your classroom. They are all made of matter. You are made of matter, too. Matter appears in three different states: solid, liquid, and gas.

Like all matter, solids take up space and have mass. Only solid matter has a shape of its own. A pencil and an eraser are both solids. Look around you and find things that are solids.

Liquid is another state of matter. It takes up space and has mass. However, it can change shape but not by itself. In fact, liquids take the shape of the container they are in. The containers could be a glass, cup, bowl, or vase. Milk, juice, and water are all liquids. If you pour a liquid from a tall glass into a round pot, what shape will it take?

The third state of matter is gas. It takes up space and has mass, but it does not have a shape of its own. Gas changes shape and size when it spreads out. Air is made up of gases. You cannot see or taste it, but you can sometimes smell it. Air is everywhere. It can fill up a balloon. What will happen to the air in a balloon if it pops?
